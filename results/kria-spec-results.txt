open as "not diagnosed" - the wart that made a battery figure and a bench figure disagree by more than

1. THE STALL: WHAT IT WAS, AND WHY THE FIX IS FREE
  The open item in kria-speed2-results.txt: "sometimes a run's shift scan costs three to eight times its
  2.7 ms norm and the whole forward wears it", hitting q20_forloop at 20.257 ms a forward, q22_nl_uitleg
  at 7.993 and q11_wordproblem at 7.850, and moving from run to run.

  IT IS NOT OUR ARITHMETIC.  In every traced forward, stalled or clean, the scan's three passes take
  0.8-1.1 ms per thread.  That number never moves.  It is not the data (the same prompt stalls in some
  repeats and not others), not the batch (it appears at --prompt-batch 1 and 4), not base 3 (it appears
  in the two-bit runs), not page faults (0-2 minor, 0 major in a stalled forward), not cgroup throttling
  (no cpu.max anywhere, nr_throttled 0) and not DVFS or heat (all four A53s pinned, die 27-30 C).

  IT IS THE BARRIERS.  (This paragraph's numbers were taken earlier in this pass with the -DPOOL_TRACE
  build, stall-trace.txt and stall-cause.txt; everything else in this file is from tonight's one session.)
  Built with -DPOOL_TRACE, the worst forward measured took 1109.9 ms of wall, of
  which the scan was 1043.0 ms; the four threads spent 966.9 / 1028.3 / 1012.4 / 961.7 ms IN THE SCAN'S
  BARRIERS and 0.9 ms each in its passes, while the scheduler said they waited 256.9 / 15.6 / 495.1 /
  328.2 ms on run queues and /proc/stat showed 4330 of 4440 core-ms busy with only 3224 of them ours.
  The shift scan is the only ARM work that runs on all four A53s behind barriers - 30 layers x 6 barriers
  = 180 barrier waits a forward - and every one of them spun on `yield` and never gave up its core.  One
  foreign runnable thread takes a core, one scan thread goes off-CPU, and the other three burn a
  scheduler slice at each of 180 barriers.  A /proc sampler named the consumer: polkitd, up to 110 ms in
  a 100 ms sample, with dbus-daemon, NetworkManager, systemd, systemd-logind and gnome-shell behind it -
  the churn an ssh login sets off.  In the quiet stretches the small stalls line up with rcu_sched,
  ksoftirqd, kworker/flush, systemd-oomd and fancontrol: 10 ms of foreign CPU bought 10-30 ms of scan.

  THE FIX, board/bitnet_kria.c only, no .v and no .tcl touched, no arithmetic touched:
    (a) prep_barrier spins BAR_SPIN = 10000 (about 30 us; a normal barrier wait measures 0.5 us) and then
        calls sched_yield instead of spinning for ever.
    (b) ffn_prep_worker: every thread now takes the four slice maxima itself and computes its own shift,
        instead of thread 0 computing it while three threads wait at a barrier for the answer.  Each
        stage keeps its own row of mx so a thread running ahead cannot overwrite a word another must
        still read.  Six barriers a layer become three and the serial stretch between each pair is gone.
  WHY IT CANNOT CHANGE A NUMBER: a maximum over the same four words is the same word in every thread, and
  stage_shift of that word is the same shift, so every slice is scaled by exactly the shift the old code
  used.  The change is who computes it, not what is computed.  The battery and the bench prove it below.

2. THE STUDY: SPECULATIVE DECODING, MEASURED AND REFUSED (speculation-study.txt, spec_study.py,
   spec_shallow.py; run on the PC, the board untouched)
  Exact speculation cannot change an id: a drafted token is accepted only when it equals the argmax the
  model would have produced there, so the emitted sequence is the greedy sequence whatever the drafter
  says.  What is not free is the verification, which costs a batched forward whether the drafts were
  right or wrong.  So the only open question was the acceptance, and it was measured on the board's own
  2097 generated ids.

  THE COST MODEL, from the runtime's own --timing lines at --prompt-batch 1, 2, 3, 4 (b3v2-batchscan.txt;
  the timers reconcile with wall clock to within 1%).  T(B) = F(B) + 4.107 + 5.983 x B, calibrated so
  that T(1) = 59.599 ms, the measured cost of a plain generated token:

4. THE ARM COST OF A BATCHED FORWARD - what a verification round would actually have paid
  A verification round is a batched forward over B positions, which is exactly the code the prompt phase
  runs.  The batch scan's per-position timers are MEASURED; the per-call split below is DERIVED from them
  by taking 57 x the printed value as the run total, subtracting the four single-position generation
  forwards, and dividing by the number of prompt calls (53, 27, 18, 14).  Milliseconds of ONE call:
    stage                            B=1       B=2       B=3       B=4    B=4 against 4 x B=1
    the shift scan                 2.317     6.837     8.573     8.658           0.934x
    norms, quant, RoPE, residuals   3.250     6.760    10.234    13.472           1.036x
    attention                      2.707     4.723     6.897     8.627           0.797x
    the sums out of the buffer     0.288     4.511     7.748    10.699           9.287x
    the two glue passes            6.848    13.442    20.145    25.925           0.946x
    the engines (weight stream)   32.721    33.421    52.044    52.016           0.397x
    the activation loads           0.879     0.997     1.122     1.223           0.348x
    the whole forward             49.509    77.374   116.798   133.159           0.672x
    ARM work in one batched call   8.562    22.830    33.453    41.456           1.210x
  READ IT LIKE THIS.  Only the weight stream amortises (0.397x at B=4, and even that is spoiled above two
  vectors).  Attention DOES share its K and V tiles and comes in sublinear at 0.797x - the grouped kernel
  does what it was built for.  The norms, the quantisation, the RoPE and the residuals are exactly linear
  (1.036x), as four independent pieces of work must be.  The glue does not batch at all (0.946x).  And
  the sums coming out of the DMA buffer are NINE TIMES what four single positions cost - 10.699 ms a call
  against 1.152 - which is the single worst line in the table and a real cost of the batched path.
  Take the sums-out stage away and the ARM work of a batched call would be 30.757 ms against 33.096 for
  four singles, 0.929x - a genuine 7% saving from attention's tile sharing.  As it stands the ARM work of
  a batch of four costs 1.210x what four single forwards cost.  THE FOUR-THREAD POOL DOES NOT RESCUE THIS:
  the pieces are already dispatched across it, and what is left is arithmetic that grows with B.
  This is the arithmetic behind the verdict.  A batch of four saves 19.7 ms of weight stream a position
  (32.721 against 52.016/4 = 13.004) and hands 9.5 ms of it straight back per round in sums-out overhead
  it would not have paid as four singles (10.699 against 4 x 0.288), on top of 25.9 ms a round of glue
  that will not batch and 13.5 of norms that cannot.

  WHICH PROMPTS GAIN AND WHICH DO NOT, plainly:
    ON A QUIET BOARD, NONE.  The quiet before-and-after columns cross each other constantly - the fixed
    binary is ahead on 16 of the 31 prompts and behind on 15 - and the battery totals (16.33 against
    16.13) put it slightly behind.  There was nothing to fix on a quiet board and the numbers say so.
    The spread runs -16.0% (q03_crc32) to +7.2% (q18_shiftreg_child), and the two worst are themselves
    stall events: q03_crc32 at 14.46 against 17.22 has a 4.877 ms scan, q04_fpga at 15.54 against 16.46
    has 4.394, against a battery median near 2.4.  That is the fix working rather than failing - after it
    a foreign core-steal costs a few milliseconds instead of tens, so it can still be seen in a per-prompt
    column but it can no longer own a run.  Note that the pre-fix binary's quiet battery caught no stall
    at all this time (its worst scan was 3.616): the wart lands at random and a quiet board may go a
    whole battery without it, which is exactly why it went undiagnosed for two passes.
    WITH A QUARTER OF AN A53 TAKEN, ALMOST EVERY PROMPT GAINS.  30 of 31 are faster with the fix, 29 of
    them by more than 10% and 27 by more than 20%, the best +66.1%; only q01_france goes the other way
    (-12.4%) and it generates 8 ids, where one scheduling event owns the average.  The scan column is the
    whole story and it has no exceptions at all: 10.9-22.7 ms a forward before, 3.2-4.9 after, lower on
    31 of 31 prompts.
    AND THE PROMPTS THE STUDY SAYS SPECULATION WOULD HAVE HELPED ARE A DIFFERENT SET ENTIRELY - q22, q11,
    q16, q10, q23 - because that list is a list of prompts whose answers repeat themselves.  Nine
    prompts would have gained nothing at all from speculation.  Neither list is the other.

9. THE NEW CEILING, AND THE NEXT LEVER
  Nothing in this pass moved the ceiling.  The token is where kria-speed2-results.txt left it -
  58.879 ms and 16.98 tok/s on the bench prompts, against 59.599 and 16.78 - and the difference is noise.
  What changed is that the floor under a disturbed board rose from 12.03 to 15.27 tok/s on the battery,
  and that the reason a battery figure and a bench figure disagreed by more than noise is now known and
  gone.  Of a 59 ms generated token the fabric still holds about 80%: the engines about 30.6 ms of weight
  stream, the glue 6.85, the head 5.39 (4.12 of it its own ternary stream); the A53s hold the rest -
  attention 6.1 at 144 positions and 11.7 at 281, norms and quantisation 3.37, the shift scan 2.34, the
  sums out 0.56.

  SPECULATION IS OFF THE LIST, and this pass is the reason: it is short of break-even by 3x to 10x with
  either draft source, and the two levers already named in kria-speed2-results.txt beat it outright.
  THE NEXT LEVER, in the order I would take it:
  1. FEWER, LONGER ENGINE RUNS - 6.70 ms a token of measured gap.  The model stream takes 32.80 ms
     against the fabric's 26.10 ms floor, and it is not the memory: the head stream, one long sequential
     read of 65,667,072 bytes, runs at 15.93-16.01 GB/s on the same DDR in the same token.  What differs
     is 120 short runs a token, each with its activation reload, register writes, descriptor setup,
     completion poll and drain.  PROJECTION: 59.6 - 6.7 = 52.9 ms a token, 18.9 tok/s.
  2. BATCHED GENERATION ACROSS INDEPENDENT CONVERSATIONS.  This is the lever speculation was a poor
     imitation of.  The engines already answer one pass of the stream for four vectors, ctrl[10:9]
     already carries the batch, and four separate conversations have NO dependency - so every position
     in the batch is a real token, always, with no acceptance risk and no wasted work.  Tonight's point-4
     table prices it honestly: at B=4 the stream falls to 0.397x but the ARM work rises to 1.210x, so the
     first thing to fix on the way is the sums-out stage (9.287x) and the glue (0.946x).  PROJECTION,
     using the same T(B) the study built: T(4) = 161.2 ms for four tokens, 40.30 ms each, 24.82 tok/s a
     conversation.  THAT NUMBER IS EXACTLY THE STUDY'S SPECULATION CEILING - the one it called
     unreachable because it assumed a drafter that is both free and always right.  Four independent
     conversations reach it without a drafter at all, because every position in the batch is a real
     token.  (kria-speed2-results.txt projected 43.2 ms and 23.1 tok/s for the same lever by a different
     route; both are projections and neither has been built.)
  3. THE GLUE, 6.85 ms a token in two passes over 6912 elements, 11.5% of a token, and the one part of
     the pipeline untouched since it was built.  It does not batch (0.946x at B=4), which makes it the
     thing standing between lever 2 and its projection.
  AND ONE MORE, ON THE SAME FRAGILITY THE STALL EXPOSED and not taken tonight: parallel_for's join loop
  still burns 8 x POOL_SPIN spins - about 1.2 ms - before it yields.  It shows in tonight's numbers.
  On q20_forloop, going from a quiet board to a quarter of an A53 taken, the FIXED runtime's attention
  rises 7.584 -> 9.318 ms a forward (+1.734) and its norms 3.373 -> 3.812 (+0.439), while its scan rises
  2.325 -> 4.587 (+2.262).  The scan is still the largest single leak but it is no longer in a class of
  its own: the join loop now costs the same order of milliseconds.  The scan was the one that mattered
  and the forward is now at its fair share of a disturbed machine, so the join loop was left alone; it is
  the next thing to bound if the board is ever expected to share its cores.
