AN EDGE DEVICE THAT ANSWERS FROM ITS OWN DATA, WITH NO NETWORK IN THE LOOP AT ALL.
microsoft/bitnet-b1.58-2B-4T, all 30 layers, every matrix product in the KV260's fabric, doing two
jobs a cloud service would need a connection for: triaging equipment alarms into strict JSON, and
reading its own sensors and reporting on itself.  Measured on 2026-09-07 between 09:40 and 11:07 UTC
on one quiet board.  Forty alarms through the fabric, the same forty through bitnet.cpp on the same
board's four A53 cores, and the same forty through the checkpoint in bf16 on an RTX 4080.

JOB 1, ALARM TRIAGE
  One line of equipment telemetry in, one line of JSON out and nothing else, so a real gateway could
  act on it without a human reading it:
    {"category": one of mechanical|electrical|thermal|sensor|network|software|none,
     "severity": one of critical|warning|info,
     "action":   one short imperative sentence for the technician}

  FORMAT.  This is where the board is flawless.
    strict JSON on the first try                    40 of 40   (json.loads on the stripped answer;
                                                                no first-{-to-last-} rescuing)
    keys exactly {category, severity, action}       40 of 40
    category inside its seven-word vocabulary       40 of 40
    severity inside its three-word vocabulary       40 of 40
    action a non-empty string                       40 of 40
    no greeting, no code fence, no second line      40 of 40
  bitnet.cpp and the RTX 4080 also scored 40 of 40 on every one of those, so the format is the
  prompt's doing, not the board's.  The prompt earns it by naming both vocabularies inline as
  literal word lists, showing the single-line shape with placeholders, forbidding the failure modes
  by name, and carrying two one-shot examples of which the second is a benign line; without a benign
  exemplar a small model narrates informational lines instead of emitting JSON for them.

  ACCURACY.  This is where the model is weak, and the weakness is not the board's.
    over the 30 clear lines     category 19/30 = 0.6333    severity 16/30 = 0.5333   both 12/30 = 0.400
    over the 10 ambiguous       category  4/10 = 0.400     severity  6/10 = 0.600
    over all 40                 category 23/40 = 0.575     severity 22/40 = 0.550
  The ground truth is the labelling agent's own reading, justified one clause per line in
  edge_alarms.json.  The ten ambiguous lines are scored separately and never folded into the
  headline.  No severity definitions were put in the system prompt: writing "critical means already
  past a trip level" would be tuning the prompt to this particular ground truth and would make the
  number dishonest.

    self02  20,960 ms, 253 prompt ids, 82 generated, 4 sentence ends, 56 words, 118.865 J

    verdict clause present on 3 of 3; 19,474 ms and 109.701 J an answer

  IS THE THREE-SENTENCE SHAPE THE BOARD OR THE MODEL?  The three self-report turns, with the board's
  own real readings in them, were handed verbatim to the checkpoint in bf16 on the RTX 4080.
    self01  identical text, IDENTICAL TOKEN FOR TOKEN (63 ids), 3 sentences both ways
    self02  different: board 4 sentences / "Nothing needs attention", GPU 3 / "Attention needed"
    self03  identical text, IDENTICAL TOKEN FOR TOKEN (66 ids), 3 sentences both ways
    sentence ends, board 3 4 3; GPU 3 3 3.  Neither ever produced two.
  So the format failure is the model's and not the board's.

  A WORSE PROBLEM THAN THE SENTENCE COUNT, and it is the honest finding of job 2: the verdict is not
  driven by the readings.  On self03 the board answered "Attention needed." on 3.82 W, a 30.9 C
  processor die, a 28.8 C fabric die, 2215 MB free and the fabric operating -- nothing on that list
  warrants attention -- and it gave no reason, although the system message asks for
  "Attention needed:" followed by one.  On self02 the board said "Nothing needs attention" and the
  RTX 4080, on the identical readings, said "Attention needed".  Two ways of running the same model
  disagreeing about the verdict on the same benign numbers is the plainest possible statement that
  the verdict carries no information.  The application records `sentences`, `words` and `verdict` on
  every reply, so this is reported rather than hidden.

  that one answer:
    on the open process        15,132.9 ms an answer

    saving                        909.9 ms an answer, 5.67 percent of end to end
    the five answers were IDENTICAL in both modes, so the saving is pure overhead, not a different
    answer.
  Two caveats.  Setup was 1.057 s warm here but 1.169 s on the first open of this session and has
  been seen at 1.52 s with a cold page cache, so the saving is a floor.  And bitnet_kria restarts
  its KV cache for every line of ids it reads, so what is being reused is the process and the 521 MB
  of weights in udmabuf0 -- not any cached context.  (An earlier build-agent measurement of the same
  thing gave 1,256.7 ms / 8.0 percent; this pass measured 909.9 ms / 5.67 percent.  Both are real;
  the difference is page-cache state on the fresh opens.)

THE OFFLINE PROOF
  The claim: nothing crossed any interface for the inference.  Networking was NOT disabled -- that
  would have cut the link to the board -- so the claim is proved by measurement, three ways.

  1  A RUN WITH NO SESSION ON THE BOARD AT ALL.  The proof run was launched with setsid+nohup and
     the launching ssh returned immediately; the script then slept 45 s so that connection's
     teardown landed outside the window, and the window was not entered again until the run was long
     over.  Ten alarms were triaged inside it.
       window   10:07:37.455Z to 10:10:21.877Z, 164.42 s
       ss -tan state established, sampled once a second for the whole window:
         0 connections on 161 of 161 samples, maximum 0
       /proc/net/dev across the window
         eth0   rx 7,715 B / 37 packets      tx 2,333 B / 24 packets
         lo       760 B / 8 packets each way
       /proc/net/dev as edge_monitor.py itself read it, around setup + 10 s idle + the ten answers
         eth0   rx 5,893 B / 31 packets      tx 1,987 B / 20 packets
       and per answer: 7 of the 10 answer windows saw at least one byte on eth0.
     Non-zero.  So every packet was captured and named.

  2  EVERY PACKET, NAMED.  tcpdump -i eth0 -p (no promiscuous mode, so this is what the board's own
     interface handled) ran across the window: 66 packets.
       17  the board SENT
       13  unicast addressed to the board
       35  broadcast and multicast from other hosts, which any NIC on the segment receives
     ALL SEVENTEEN THE BOARD SENT:
        5  DNS to the LAN resolver 89.101.251.228 -- four of them A and AAAA for the board's own
           hostname kria.dynamic.ziggo.nl, fired at window open + 0.018 s, which is the instant the
           proof script ran its own snapshot commands and about twelve seconds BEFORE bitnet_kria
           was started at all, so whichever of those commands asked for it, it cannot be inference;
           and one AAAA for connectivity-check.ubuntu.com
        4  ARP (who-has the gateway, and replies to the gateway asking for the board)
        4  TCP to 2620:2d:4002:1::197 port 80 -- NetworkManager's periodic connectivity check: SYN,
           ack, "GET / HTTP/1.1", ack.  Canonical answered "HTTP/1.1 204 No Content".
        2  ICMP echo replies to the router pinging the board
        1  IPv6 neighbour advertisement answering the router's solicitation
        1  IGMP v3 membership report
     Not one of the seventeen is the inference.  Two of them leave the LAN: a DNS lookup and
     NetworkManager's connectivity check, both the operating system's own housekeeping, both on a
     schedule that has nothing to do with the model.  The interface the answers would have used is
     eth0 -- it and lo are the only interfaces on this board -- and what was counted is the whole of
     /proc/net/dev's rx_bytes/tx_bytes/rx_packets/tx_packets for eth0, plus every packet tcpdump saw
     on it.

  3  THE DECISIVE ONE: THE INFERENCE HOLDS NO SOCKET.  Every open file descriptor of every process
     in the chain, listed while it was answering:
       bitnet_kria (this is the process that runs every matrix product in the fabric) -- 10 fds:
         0 -> pipe          (stdin from the python parent)
         1 -> pipe          (stdout to the python parent)
         2 -> the log file
         3 -> /dev/udmabuf0     4 -> /dev/udmabuf0
         5 -> .../udmabuf0/sync_for_cpu
         6 -> /dev/udmabuf1     7 -> /dev/udmabuf1
         8 -> .../udmabuf1/sync_for_cpu
         9 -> /dev/mem
         NOT ONE SOCKET.  There is no descriptor through which it could reach a network.
       python3 edge_monitor.py -- 5 fds: a stdin pipe, stdout and stderr both to the log file, and
         the two pipes it talks to bitnet_kria through.  No socket.
       sudo, the launcher -- 2 sockets, and both were resolved against the kernel's own tables:
         inode 3919053 is in /proc/net/netlink, protocol 9 = NETLINK_AUDIT (sudo writing the audit
         record), inode 3919054 is an unnamed local socketpair.  NEITHER appears in /proc/net/tcp,
         /proc/net/tcp6, /proc/net/udp or /proc/net/udp6 -- the kernel's complete tables of IPv4 and
         IPv6 sockets -- so neither can put a packet on a wire.
     Forty process samples were taken two seconds apart across a run: 26 showed 0 sockets and 14
     showed 2.  Every one of the 14 was the sudo launcher.  bitnet_kria and edge_monitor.py showed
     0 sockets on every sample in which they appeared.
     (The fd listing above is one process sampled in full and shows bitnet_kria holding 10; the
     forty-sample census was a separate invocation and counted 11 or 12 fds for it, because that one
     was logging to a file it also kept open.  The number that matters is the same in both: zero
     sockets, on every sample of both.)

  WHAT THE MAIN 53-ANSWER RUN'S OWN COUNTERS SAY, and why they are not the proof.  Across that run
  eth0 moved 70,776 B in and 39,164 B out as the application itself bracketed it (net_before to
  net_after in edge-run.json), and 72,416 B in / 42,024 B out over the slightly wider bracket of
  edge-net-before.txt to edge-net-after.txt, which were taken from outside the application and so
  also contain the launching session.  That is not zero and it is not claimed to be:
  that run was launched over ssh and its completion was waited on, so a control connection existed
  around it, and no tcpdump was taken over that window to attribute the bytes packet by packet.
  That is exactly why a separate run was made with no session in existence at all, and it is the
  one above that carries the claim.  Quoting the main run's counters as an offline proof would be
  dishonest, so they are quoted here as what they are.

  The weights are read once from the SD card into udmabuf0 at process start and the fabric streams
  them from DDR thereafter; the tokenizer is a local file; the sensors are sysfs.  There is no step
  in either job that has anywhere to go.

WHAT THIS BOARD COULD AND COULD NOT BE USED FOR AT THE EDGE, on this evidence
  IT COULD, today, as built:
    - Emit machine-usable structured output with no server.  Forty of forty answers were strict JSON
      with exactly the right keys and both fields inside their vocabularies, first try, greedy, and
      the two baselines confirm the prompt rather than the board is what earns that.  A gateway
      could parse and route these without a human.
    - Sort alarms into the right SUBSYSTEM most of the time, at 14.5 s and 79.5 J an answer on a
      5.5 W board with the network unplugged.  Mechanical 7/8, electrical 5/6, network 4/4.  As a
      first-pass router -- "this is an electrical problem, page the electricians" -- it works.
    - Do it faster and on a fifth of the energy of the same board's own CPUs, which is the whole
      argument for putting the model in the fabric: 5.34x the speed at 5.04x fewer joules.
    - Read its own sensors and quote them back accurately in English.  Every number in every
      self-report was correct.
    - Run with genuinely nothing leaving the device.  The inference process holds ten file
      descriptors and not one of them is a socket.
  IT COULD NOT, on this evidence:
    - Be trusted with SEVERITY.  One "critical" in forty lines, and twelve of thirteen genuinely
      critical faults downgraded to warning or info.  Anything downstream that acts on severity --
      paging, shutdown interlocks, escalation -- would sleep through an earth fault that had already
      opened a breaker.  This is the model, not the board: the RTX 4080 downgrades the same twelve.
    - Be trusted to catch INSTRUMENT faults.  Sensor category 1 of 6 over all forty lines and 0 of
      the 4 clear ones.  A transmitter frozen at the 4.00 mA rail for 47 minutes while the process
      moved 12 K was returned as none/info by all three ways.
    - Have its ACTION field executed.  Three of the fourteen transcribed answers give advice that is
      wrong or dangerous: reopen a breaker on an unresolved earth fault, reduce a load to the value
      it already has, run the brake test that just failed.  The action sentence is fine as a hint to
      a technician who reads it; it is not an instruction to a machine.
    - Be used where an answer must arrive quickly.  14.5 s an answer, three quarters of it prompt
      eval, means about four alarms a minute.  A burst of alarms from one plant trip would queue.
    - Produce a fixed-length reply.  Two sentences were asked for and three came back, on every way
      of running the model.
  THE HONEST SUMMARY.  As an offline structured-output front end -- take a line of telemetry, return
  a parseable object, name the subsystem, keep the plant's data on the plant -- this board does the
  job at 5.5 W with nothing on the wire.  As a judge of how urgent something is, it does not, and no
  amount of fabric fixes that: the same weights on a 4080 are exactly as wrong.  What would fix it
  is a bigger model or severity rules in the prompt, and the second was deliberately not done here
  because it would have been tuning the prompt to this file's own answer key.

METHOD NOTES, so the raw files can be checked against this one without surprise
  1  edge-run.json's OWN summary block does not match the headline, and should not.  It reports 50
     answers and "clear 40" with category 0.725 and severity 0.600, because edge_monitor.py folds
     the ten answers of the --saving pass back in and counts every non-ambiguous answer, repeats
     included, as clear.  The headline in this file is score_edge.py's, which scores each of the
     forty alarms once, excludes every mode-tagged (shared/fresh) row, and applies one scoring rule
     to all three ways.  Those are the numbers to quote: 19/30, 16/30, 12/30.
  2  Generated-id counts for the self-reports are quoted with the end-of-turn token excluded (63,
     82, 66) so they line up with what the RTX 4080 reports; edge-run.json stores the raw counts
     (64, 83, 67).  The triage counts have no such adjustment.
  3  bitnet.cpp's prompt is exactly one token longer on all forty lines because llama.cpp prepends
     a BOS the other two ways do not.  That is the only difference between the three inputs.
  4  quality_bitnetcpp.py gained two additive fields (t0, t1) so each llama-cli invocation's INA260
     window could be integrated exactly as edge_monitor integrates the board's.  No behaviour of
     the baseline changed.
  5  The offline-proof window was run three times.  The first two were contaminated by a wait
     connection opened inside the window itself, which is exactly the thing the window is supposed
     to exclude; both were discarded and the clean third run is the one reported here.  Nothing
     from the discarded runs is quoted.
  6  bitnet.cpp's 5.1547 W is the trapezoid mean over its whole 3,108.9 s window.  Dividing its
     total 16,022.1 J by the 3,108.1 s it spent answering gives 5.1549 W, and the plain arithmetic
     mean of the 60,822 in-window samples gives 5.1544 W; the three agree to four figures.
  7  Every number in this file is measured.  The one projection is marked as such in POWER.
