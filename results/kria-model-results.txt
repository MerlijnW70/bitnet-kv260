FIVE THINGS THAT WERE WRONG WITH THE MEASUREMENT AND WERE FIXED BEFORE ANY NUMBER WAS KEPT
  1. polkitd was spinning at 88.9% of one A53 (9:42 of CPU) when the first idle sample was taken. It would have
     charged a busy core to every number here. `sudo systemctl restart polkit` stopped it; every measurement below
     was taken after that, with `top -bn1` showing no process above 12%.
  2. The sampler of board/power-ffn.sh is a shell loop that forks `date` and three `cat`s every 50 ms: 80 forks a
     second on four A53s. Measured A/B on the SAME run (the like-for-like prompt, 16 ids, 80 generated, 4 threads):
       with the forking loop     6.92 tok/s generated, head 68.431 ms a call, engines 44.752 ms a forward
       with one long-lived process 8.00 tok/s generated, head 52.557 ms a call, engines 43.146 ms a forward
     and the idle it reports is 0.13 W higher (3.609 W over a 40 s standalone sample against 3.482 W). The loop was
     replaced by board/ina260-sample.py, one process that keeps the three sysfs files open and seeks them; all the
     power numbers below use it. The forking loop's own cost is 13.5% of the generation rate and 30% of the head.
  3. The first bitnet.cpp power run was cold: llama's own "load time = 78966.16 ms" off the SD card filled the
     window and pulled its mean to 3.983 W. Re-run warm (load time 2727.24 ms) it reads 4.870 W over the window.
  4. board/power-bitnetcpp.sh's first grep for llama's timings missed them: this build prefixes every line with
     "0.41.447.893 I common_perf_print:", so "^ *eval time" never matched. The pattern is now "eval time".
  5. The whole-window mean of a llama run is NOT its generation power: the window holds a 6 s start and load at
     3.72 W, the prompt eval at 4.89 W and only then the generation at 5.14 W. The trace was segmented by counting
     llama's own reported prompt-eval and eval milliseconds backwards from the end of the high-power region
     (segments.py); the segment boundaries it finds land on the steps visible in the one-second trace.

WHERE THE TIME GOES, AND WHAT WOULD MOVE IT (the two largest items are both at a measured ceiling)
  Of a generated token's 124.9 ms: the engines 43.06 ms (34.5%), the head 52.11 ms (41.7%), attention 7.68 ms,
  norms/quant/RoPE/residuals 7.14 ms, the two glue passes 6.83 ms, the shift scan 2.78 ms, sums out 2.13 ms,
  activation loads 1.08 ms, waiting on the side thread 0.10 ms. Those parts add to 122.9 ms; the other 2.0 ms is
  what the forward's own clock counts outside the timed stages (DMA arming, control writes, status polls).
  The engines are on the DDR read path's own limit (12.10 GB/s against the 12.14-12.25 GB/s matvec4-results.txt
  measured through the same four ports); 521 MB have to cross it once a token whatever runs above them.
  The head is NEON-compute-bound, not DDR-bound: it reaches 6.30 GB/s where the engines reach 12.10, it scales
  almost linearly with cores (the generate agent measured 189.13 ms at one thread against 49.57 at four), and two
  other kernels measured on the board were slower (an explicit prefetch 73.00 ms, two rows a load 71.98 ms).
  PROJECTION, marked as a projection and not a run: an int8 head in the fabric at the engines' measured 12.10 GB/s
  would take 328335360 / 12.10e9 = 27.1 ms instead of 52.11, which would put a generated token at 99.9 ms =
  10.0 tok/s, and at the same 5.5 W that is 0.55 J a token. Nothing of that is built or measured.
