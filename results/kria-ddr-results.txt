The DDR read ceiling of the KV260's four ternary matvec engines, measured 2026-09-06: what raised it and
what did not.  The fabric reads weights at 12.19 GB/s before and 13.33 GB/s after (+9.4%), a generated
token's engine time falls 43.45 -> 39.59 ms, generation 13.05 -> 13.87 tokens/s and a prompt token
15.88 -> 16.95, and all 2097 generated ids of the 31-prompt battery are byte-identical to the baseline's.

WHAT CHANGED, AND WHAT DID NOT
  Changed: board/build-ffn.tcl only, two parameters on all five AXI DMAs (the four engines' and the
  glue's), nothing else in the design:
      c_s2mm_burst_size        16 -> 256    a write carries 256 beats of sums, not 16
      c_m_axi_s2mm_data_width  32 -> 128    and each beat is 16 bytes, not 4
  so the sums leave in writes of up to 4096 bytes instead of 64, and the DDR turns the bus around
  between reads and writes some sixty times less often.
  The sum STREAM is still 32 bits (c_s_axis_s2mm_tdata_width 32), so ternary_matvec.v, ternary_glue.v,
  mul16.v, the shells, the stream formats, the control and status words, the register map and the
  device-tree overlay are all untouched.  No software change: board/bitnet_kria.c, ternary_matvec.c,
  ternary_ffn.c, bitnet_chat.py, quality_board.py and runbench.py are the same files, and the binaries
  measured before and after are the same binary.
  Not changed, because measured and rejected: the MM2S max burst (64 -> 256 does nothing, twice) and the
  PS port arrangement (engines on HP0 HP2 HPC0 HPC1 instead of HP0..HP3 is 6.4% SLOWER).
  Constraint the wider S2MM adds (met everywhere today, worth keeping in mind): the DMA has no DRE, so
  each engine's sum buffer must start on a 16-byte boundary.  In bitnet_kria.c every sum buffer is a
  4096-byte-aligned offset plus i * neurons_per_engine * 4, and neurons_per_engine is 960 (qkv), 640 (o),
  3456 (gate+up), 640 (down) and 32064 (head) -- all multiples of 4, so every start is 16-byte aligned.
  In ternary_matvec.c it is i * 6912 and in ternary_ffn.c the same shape.

WHAT WAS TRIED, IN ORDER (4 engines, 4423680 weight bytes = 276480 beats, board/ternary_matvec.c's
benchmark, one core pinned to the driver: sudo taskset -c 0 ./ternary_matvec --engines 4 --runs N)
  Every line below is the driver's own summary line, verbatim; "0 of 6912 wrong" in every run of every set.

  1. THE BASELINE (MM2S burst 64, S2MM burst 16, S2MM memory port 32 bits, engines on HP0..HP3, glue HPC0)
     weight phase over 2000 runs on 4 engines: mean 0.363 ms (12.175 GB/s, 3.044 GB/s a stream), min 0.357 ms (12.379 GB/s, 3.095 GB/s a stream); whole matvec mean 0.371 ms, min 0.365 ms

  2. (a) MM2S MAX BURST 64 -> 256 BEATS.  NOTHING.  (build-ffn.tcl name ffn256 burst 256)
     weight phase over 2000 runs on 4 engines: mean 0.363 ms (12.196 GB/s, 3.049 GB/s a stream), min 0.357 ms (12.380 GB/s, 3.095 GB/s a stream); whole matvec mean 0.371 ms, min 0.365 ms
     +0.2%, inside the run-to-run spread.  Scaling identical (E=1 3.959, E=2 7.896, E=3 11.608 GB/s).
     The change did reach the pins: in the generated work_<name>/.../design_1.bd the five SmartConnect
     masters into the PS carry MAX_BURST_LENGTH 64 for the baseline and 256 with burst 256 (the shipped
     design carries 256 too, there because of the S2MM burst), and dma0 grew 1620 -> 1656 LUTs and
     2461 -> 2477 FFs.  Tried again on top of the winning
     S2MM change (name ffnall burst 256 sburst 256 swidth 128) and it is still nothing:
     weight phase over 20000 runs on 4 engines: mean 0.331 ms (13.364 GB/s, 3.341 GB/s a stream), min 0.321 ms (13.765 GB/s, 3.441 GB/s a stream); whole matvec mean 0.339 ms, min 0.329 ms
     against 13.326 GB/s for the same design with the burst left at 64.  So the shipped design keeps 64:
     a longer read burst is not what the DDR path is short of.

  3. (b) THE PORT ARRANGEMENT.  WORSE.  (name ffnspread ports spread: engines on HP0, HP2, HPC0, HPC1,
     glue on HP1, so the engines' reads are split over the two AFI pairs AND the CCI)
     weight phase over 2000 runs on 4 engines: mean 0.388 ms (11.398 GB/s, 2.849 GB/s a stream), min 0.380 ms (11.651 GB/s, 2.913 GB/s a stream); whole matvec mean 0.396 ms, min 0.388 ms
     -6.4% against the baseline.  Where it goes wrong is the fourth engine, not the third: E=1 3.960,
     E=2 (HP0+HP2) 7.894, E=3 (+HPC0) 11.562 -- all within 0.5% of the HP-only design -- and only
     E=4 (+HPC1) falls, 12.19 -> 11.40.  A coherent port is as good as an AFI port for one stream and
     the second one costs.
     Which HP ports the engines use does not matter at all below the ceiling: two engines reach
     7.890-7.897 GB/s on every pair, measured on the baseline bitstream by moving the driver's base
     and stride ({0,1} 7.896, {0,2} 7.890, {0,3} 7.896, {1,2} 7.894, {2,3} 7.897 GB/s), so HP0/HP1 and
     HP2/HP3 do not share a limit that two engines can reach.  Today's HP0..HP3 is the best of the
     arrangements the PS offers.

  4. THE SUMS' WRITES ARE WHAT COSTS.  S2MM BURST 16 -> 256.  +7.4%.  (name ffns256 sburst 256)
     weight phase over 20000 runs on 4 engines: mean 0.338 ms (13.090 GB/s, 3.272 GB/s a stream), min 0.330 ms (13.387 GB/s, 3.347 GB/s a stream); whole matvec mean 0.346 ms, min 0.339 ms
     What pointed at it: on the baseline bitstream the same 4423680 weight bytes ran at two different
     rates depending only on how many 32-bit sums came back with them.  board/ternary_ffn.c, token 2,
     five runs, verbatim:
       token 2 over 5 runs: gate+up weight phase mean 0.734 ms (12.048 GB/s), min 0.726 ms (12.189 GB/s); down weight phase mean 0.341 ms (12.969 GB/s), min 0.339 ms (13.037 GB/s)
     gate+up is 8847360 bytes for 13824 sums (one sum per 640 bytes), down is 4423680 bytes for 2560
     sums (one per 1728) -- and down runs 7.6% faster.  With 64-byte writes (16 beats x 32 bits) the
     DDR turns the bus around thousands of times a matvec; with 1024-byte writes it hardly notices.
     After the change the two converge (same command, token 2): gate+up min 0.688 ms (12.866 GB/s),
     down min 0.339 ms (13.046 GB/s).

     weight phase over 2000 runs on 4 engines: mean 0.331 ms (13.367 GB/s, 3.342 GB/s a stream), min 0.326 ms (13.585 GB/s, 3.396 GB/s a stream); whole matvec mean 0.339 ms, min 0.334 ms

WHAT IS LEFT
  Of a 72.10 ms generated token the fabric now streams for 45.6 ms (engines 39.6, head stage 1 6.0) and
  the A53s work for the rest.  The read path is at 13.33 GB/s against the 14.02 GB/s the DDR has been
  measured to hand out in total, so at most 5% more is available by pushing the memory path, and the
  four AXI pipes' 16.0 GB/s is no longer the binding limit either -- the DRAM is.
  The one lever left on the fabric side is the traffic itself, plan point 4(c): five trits a byte in base
  3 (243 of 256 values) is 1.6 bits a weight instead of 2, so a beat would carry 80 weights instead of 64
  and a token's 521.0 MB would become 417.5 MB (K = 2560 is 32 beats exactly; K = 6912 needs 87 beats,
  6960 weights, 48 of them zero padding).  PROJECTION, not a run: at the measured 13.33 GB/s the engine
  phases would fall 39.59 -> 31.7 ms and the head's stage 1 5.98 -> 4.8, so a generated token 72.1 ->
  63.0 ms and generation 13.87 -> 15.9 tokens/s.  It costs a real engine change (a 256-entry decode table
  a byte, 80 lanes instead of 64, a seven-level adder tree, a 640-bit activation store word), a repacked
  model.bin and head_t.bin, a runtime that knows the new beat counts, a rebuild and a full revalidation;
  it is exact by construction (the same ternary weights, the same integer sums), so the ids should not
  move, but nothing of it is measured here.
  Not tried: the DDRC's own port priorities and QoS registers (they are PS registers, not a bitstream
  change); a second pl_clk to run the engines faster (pointless while a stream sits at 0.83 beats a clock
  waiting for DDR).
